# Bearing Failure Modes

## 1. Rolling Contact Fatigue (Spalling / Pitting)
**Description:** Sub-surface initiated cracks propagate to the surface under cyclic rolling contact
stresses, producing pits and flakes (spalls) on raceways or rolling elements.

**Sensor signatures:**
- Elevated vibration at bearing defect frequencies (BPFO, BPFI, BSF, FTF).
- Increasing kurtosis and crest factor in the vibration time series.
- Gradual bearing temperature rise.
- Increased ultrasonic emission (dB level trending upward).

**Causes:**
- Fatigue life reached (normal end-of-life).
- Overloading beyond rated capacity.
- Misalignment introducing uneven stress distribution.
- Inadequate lubrication film causing metal-to-metal contact.

**Progression:** Early spalling shows high-frequency impulse energy; advanced spalling raises
the noise floor across a wide frequency band.

**Recommended Action:** Schedule planned replacement once defect frequencies appear; replace
immediately if vibration exceeds 50% over baseline.

---

## 2. Lubrication Failure
**Description:** Insufficient or degraded lubricant film causes adhesive wear, smearing, and
ultimately surface damage on raceways and rolling elements.

**Sensor signatures:**
- Rapid temperature rise at bearing housing.
- Elevated ultrasonic noise (dB spike) before vibration increase.
- Discolouration of grease (darkening, burning smell).

**Causes:**
- Re-greasing interval missed.
- Wrong grease type or viscosity grade.
- Grease contaminated with water or particles.
- High temperature exceeding grease rated temperature.

**Recommended Action:** Re-grease and monitor. If temperature does not fall within 30 minutes
after re-greasing, schedule immediate replacement.

---

## 3. Contamination (False Brinelling / True Brinelling / Abrasive Wear)
**Description:** Particles or water entering the bearing cause abrasive wear; fretting corrosion
and indentations result from vibration while stationary (false brinelling) or static overload
(true brinelling).

**Sensor signatures:**
- Elevated broadband vibration floor.
- Irregular impulse pattern not locked to defect frequencies.
- Rust-coloured grease or metallic debris on re-lubrication.

**Causes:**
- Damaged or inadequate seals.
- Improper storage (vibration during transit).
- Static overload during shipping or installation.

**Recommended Action:** Inspect seals; replace bearing; improve contamination exclusion measures.

---

## 4. Misalignment
**Description:** Angular or parallel shaft misalignment introduces non-uniform load distribution
across the bearing width, accelerating fatigue in loaded zones.

**Sensor signatures:**
- Elevated 1× and 2× running-speed vibration.
- Asymmetric temperature distribution across the bearing housing.
- Uneven wear patterns on raceways visible during inspection.

**Causes:**
- Improper installation alignment.
- Foundation settling.
- Thermal growth differential.
- Bent shaft.

**Recommended Action:** Correct alignment and re-baseline vibration; replace bearing if fatigue
damage is present.

---

## 5. Electrical Fluting (EDM Damage)
**Description:** Stray or induced currents arc through the bearing, leaving crater pits on
raceways that eventually produce a regular corrugation (fluting) pattern.

**Sensor signatures:**
- Regular corrugation pattern (fluting) visible on raceway surface during inspection.
- Elevated broadband vibration with a characteristic washboard pattern.
- Grey-black pitting on raceways; burnt or metallic grease.

**Causes:**
- Variable frequency drive (VFD) shaft voltages without proper bearing isolation.
- Faulty grounding.
- Welding currents passing through the bearing.

**Recommended Action:** Install insulated bearings (on DE or NDE), shaft grounding rings,
or hybrid ceramic bearings on the drive-end; verify grounding and bonding.
