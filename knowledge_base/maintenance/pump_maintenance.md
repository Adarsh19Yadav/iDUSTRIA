# Pump Maintenance Guide

## Overview
Centrifugal and positive-displacement pumps are critical assets in process industries.
Regular maintenance prevents seal failures, cavitation damage, and flow loss.

## Inspection Intervals
- **Low-criticality pumps:** inspect every 2 000 operating hours or semi-annually.
- **High-criticality pumps (continuous duty, hazardous fluid):** inspect every 1 000 operating
  hours or quarterly.

## Mechanical Seal Maintenance
- Inspect mechanical seal flush plans (API Plan 11, 21, 32, etc.) for correct flow and pressure.
- Check barrier/buffer fluid level and condition for dual seals (API Plan 52/53).
- A sudden increase in seal leakage rate beyond the allowable threshold requires planned
  replacement at the next maintenance window; major leakage requires immediate shutdown.
- Mechanical seal faces must be replaced as a matched set; never reuse a worn seat with new faces.

## Vibration and Noise
- Cavitation produces a crackling or gravel-like noise and elevated broadband vibration.
  Corrective actions: increase suction head, reduce flow rate, check for suction strainer blockage.
- Recirculation (operation well below BEP) causes low-frequency pressure pulsations; operate
  closer to Best Efficiency Point (BEP) where possible.
- Bearing frequencies in the vibration spectrum that trend upward indicate bearing deterioration
  and warrant lubrication check followed by planned replacement.

## Performance Monitoring
- Trend differential pressure (head) and flow rate together; deviation from the pump curve
  indicates wear, fouling, or recirculation issues.
- Track power consumption: increased power at the same operating point suggests internal wear
  or increased fluid viscosity.
- Efficiency loss > 5% from baseline warrants an impeller inspection.

## Temperature Limits
- Pump casing and bearing temperatures should remain within manufacturer specifications.
- High bearing temperature (> 90 °C for standard ball bearings unless otherwise specified)
  indicates lubrication or loading problems.
- Excessive fluid temperature reduces NPSH available; check suction conditions.

## Coupling and Alignment
- Misalignment is a leading cause of pump bearing and seal failures.
- Re-check alignment after any maintenance event involving disassembly.
- Soft-foot and pipe strain must be eliminated before final alignment is verified.

## Corrective Actions by Symptom
| Symptom | Likely Cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Noise + high vibration | Cavitation | Check suction conditions; increase NPSH |
| Reduced flow at same head | Worn impeller or air ingestion | Inspect impeller; check for air leaks |
| Seal leakage increase | Seal wear or flush plan failure | Check flush plan; replace seal |
| Motor overload | Fluid density change or internal rub | Investigate and correct before re-start |
| Elevated bearing temperature | Lubrication or misalignment | Re-grease or re-align |

## Spare Parts
- Keep a ready-spare mechanical seal kit and bearing set for every critical pump class.
- Impeller wear rings should be replaced when clearance exceeds twice the original specification.
