# Bearing Maintenance Guide

## Overview
Rolling-element bearings are among the most common components in rotating machinery.
Proper maintenance practices extend service life and prevent unplanned downtime.

## Inspection Intervals
- **Light duty (< 2 000 rpm, clean environment):** inspect every 2 000 operating hours or annually.
- **Moderate duty (2 000–5 000 rpm):** inspect every 1 000 operating hours or semi-annually.
- **Heavy duty (> 5 000 rpm or contaminated environment):** inspect every 500 operating hours or quarterly.

## Lubrication
- Re-grease according to the equipment manufacturer's interval; a common starting point is every
  500–2 000 hours depending on speed and load.
- Use the grease type specified for the operating temperature range. Mixing incompatible greases
  causes premature degradation.
- Over-greasing is as harmful as under-greasing: excess grease generates heat and churning losses.
- For oil-lubricated bearings, change oil at manufacturer-specified intervals or when analysis
  shows high particulate count or degraded viscosity.

## Vibration Monitoring
- Establish a vibration baseline during commissioning.
- ISO 10816 / ISO 20816 provide general vibration severity guidelines for rotating machinery.
- A 25% increase over baseline in the bearing defect frequency bands (BPFO, BPFI, BSF, FTF)
  warrants investigation.
- Ultrasonic monitoring (dB level trending) is effective for detecting early-stage grease
  starvation and surface fatigue.

## Temperature Limits
- Normal operating temperature for grease-lubricated bearings is typically 60–80 °C (140–176 °F).
- Sustained temperatures above 100 °C (212 °F) degrade most mineral-oil greases rapidly.
- A sudden temperature rise of more than 15 °C above trend baseline is an alert condition.

## Corrective Actions by Severity
| Vibration Level | Action |
|---|---|
| +10–25% over baseline | Increase monitoring frequency |
| +25–50% over baseline | Schedule planned replacement within next maintenance window |
| > 50% over baseline | Immediate shutdown and replacement |

## Common Failure Signs
- Rumbling or grinding noise at bearing frequencies
- Elevated skin temperature at bearing housing
- Visible rust, pitting, or spalling on raceways
- Excessive radial or axial play beyond manufacturer tolerance

## Replacement Notes
- Always replace the bearing with one of equal or superior specification.
- Clean and inspect the housing bore and shaft seat for damage before installation.
- Use proper fitting tools; never apply force through the rolling elements.
- Record installation date and lot number in the maintenance log.
